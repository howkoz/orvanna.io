Build an ensemble. Build a team.

Orvanna sells artificial intelligence agents by subscription. A Conductor runs an ensemble of those agents and helps other people do the same. This document explains, with the arithmetic in plain sight, exactly how a Conductor is paid for that.

Part one is the plan that runs today. Part two is a two-layer extension that has been modelled, verified and attacked, and has never paid anybody.

Demonstration document

Orvanna is a demonstration company and its Conductor program is a working prototype. Member records and the organisation tree are synthetic; the purchases behind them are real orders placed through the live storefront using sandbox test cards. No real money moves at any point, so every figure here is real to the demonstration's commission engine and to nothing else.

Not an income claim. The worked examples exist to show how the arithmetic runs. They are illustrations, not projections, and nothing here promises or implies that any person would earn any amount.

Part one · the plan that runs today
Section one

How a Conductor earns, in one paragraph

A Conductor subscribes to one or more Orvanna agents, and every agent carries a Personal Volume figure equal to its price in dollars. Everything bought on that account in a calendar month adds up to their Sales Volume, and 80 percent of that becomes Commissionable Volume, which is the only number any commission is ever calculated from. Each month the company walks up the sponsorship tree from every buyer and pays a slice of that buyer's Commissionable Volume to the Conductors above them: 10 percent to the direct sponsor, then 5, 5, 3 and 2 percent to the next four up. Two gates decide whether a given Conductor actually receives their slice. First, they must be qualified, meaning their own Sales Volume that month was 100.00 or more. Second, the level must sit inside their paid depth, which their rank sets: a Member reaches one level down, a Builder two, a Leader three, a Director four, an Executive five. Anything that fails either gate is paid to nobody. That is the whole earning model — one mechanism, two gates, no bonus pools, no matching bonuses, no fast start, and no rebate on your own purchases.

Then two gates decide who receives it. Gate A: the earner is qualified — their own Sales Volume was 100.00 or more. Gate B: the level sits inside their paid depth, which their rank sets. Anything that fails either gate is paid to nobody. The plan calls that breakage, and it is not redistributed, not pushed upward, and not held for later.

Sections two and three

The two roles, and every word defined

Two roles exist in this plan. A Conductor has enrolled and holds a place in the sponsorship tree: they can qualify, hold rank and earn. A customer subscribes through a referring Conductor without joining the tree: they never qualify, never hold rank and never earn, but their volume books onto the Conductor's account.

One word does a lot of work. Your ensemble is the set of agents you subscribe to — what you buy. Your team is the set of Conductors beneath you in the tree — who you sponsored, and who they sponsored. Both get called "yours" in conversation, and they are paid on completely differently: your ensemble opens your gate and pays you nothing; your team is where every dollar comes from.

Personal Volume (PV) What an agent carries, equal to its price in dollars. A $100 agent is 100 PV.
Sales Volume (SV) Everything bought on your account in a calendar month — your own subscriptions plus your customers’ orders.
Commissionable Volume (CV) Eighty percent of Sales Volume. The only number a commission rate is ever applied to.
Team Volume (TV) The Sales Volume of your whole organisation, excluding your own.
Qualified month Your own Sales Volume reached 100.00. Gate A. Nothing is paid to you without it.
Paid depth How many levels below you your pay reaches. Set by rank. Gate B.
Active leg A frontline Conductor who recorded 100.00 Sales Volume in the month.

Sections four and five

Volume, the eighty percent rule, and the single gate

Volume and dollars are the same number: a $100 agent carries 100 PV, a $50 agent carries 50 PV. Everything bought on your account in a calendar month — your own subscriptions plus your customers' — sums to your Sales Volume. Eighty percent of it becomes Commissionable Volume. Nothing in this plan is ever calculated from the price.

The ceiling, which is the plan's central promise. The five level rates sum to 25 percent of Commissionable Volume, and Commissionable Volume is 80 percent of the price. So no order can ever pay out more than 20 percent of its price, whatever the rates are set to. The ceiling is the arithmetic the amounts are computed by, not a limit somebody watches afterward. It has no exception.

The qualified month

One gate governs whether you are paid at all: your own Sales Volume for the month must reach 100.00. Exactly 100.00 qualifies — every threshold in this plan is inclusive. Customer volume counts toward it, so a Conductor who personally bought nothing can still qualify on two customer sales. A month that misses the gate pays you nothing and banks nothing.

The gate is about you, not about the people below you. If somebody in your team is unqualified, their volume still pays you in full. Their own earnings stop; yours do not.

Section seven · earning one

Your frontline, at ten percent

This is where most of the money in the plan is made, because level 1 pays 10 percent — twice the rate of any other level. You are paid 10 percent of the Commissionable Volume of every Conductor you personally sponsored, for as long as you are qualified that month. Their own qualification does not matter. If they bought, you are paid on it.

Worked, every step

M2 sponsored M6. This month M6 subscribed to the Marketing Agent and the Customer Care agent.

  1. Marketing Agent $100.00, 100 PV. Customer Care agent $50.00, 50 PV.
  2. M6's Sales Volume is 100 + 50 = 150.00.
  3. M6's Commissionable Volume is 0.80 × 150.00 = 120.00.
  4. M2 is qualified, and level 1 sits inside every rank's paid depth, so both gates pass.
  5. M2's commission line is 0.10 × 120.00 = 12.00.

That is $12.00 on $150.00 of buying — 8 percent of what M6 actually spent, not 10, because the 10 percent applies to Commissionable Volume.

When the person below is not qualified. M8 sponsored M10, who subscribed to the Secretary agent only: Sales Volume 50.00, Commissionable Volume 40.00. M10 is not qualified. That does not matter here — M8 is qualified, so M8's level 1 line is 0.10 × 40.00 = 4.00. M10 earns nothing and M10's leg does not count as active for M8's rank, but M10's volume still pays M8 in full. That asymmetry is deliberate: all Commissionable Volume pays upline, whoever generated it.

Section eight · earning two

Depth, which rank unlocks

Levels 2 through 5 pay 5, 5, 3 and 2 percent of that person's Commissionable Volume, and you only reach them if your rank says you do. Paid depth is a hard cut: at depth 2 you are paid on levels 1 and 2 and you see nothing from level 3, however much volume is sitting there.

Worked, every step

M3 is a Builder, so M3's paid depth is 2. M7 and M8 sit on M3's frontline, and M10 one level further down beneath M8.

  1. M7 holds twelve domain agents and six support agents: Sales Volume (12 × 100) + (6 × 50) = 1,500.00, Commissionable Volume 1,200.00.
  2. M8 holds two support agents: Sales Volume 100.00, Commissionable Volume 80.00.
  3. M10 holds one support agent: Sales Volume 50.00, Commissionable Volume 40.00.
  4. M7 at level 1: 0.10 × 1,200.00 = 120.00. M8 at level 1: 0.10 × 80.00 = 8.00. M10 at level 2: 0.05 × 40.00 = 2.00.
  5. M3's total is 120.00 + 8.00 + 2.00 = 130.00.

Now change one thing and nothing else. Suppose M3 held Member instead of Builder, so paid depth 1. M10 is out of reach. M3 earns 120.00 + 8.00 = 128.00, and the 2.00 is breakage. It is not pushed to the person above and not held for later. Nobody receives it.

Rank buys reach, not rate. Going from Member to Builder does not increase what you earn on your frontline by a single cent. It opens a new row of people underneath them.

Section nine · earning three

Customer sales rolling up

Anybody can subscribe to Orvanna agents as a customer of a referring Conductor without joining the tree. That order books onto the Conductor's account, tagged as a customer purchase with the customer's own identity kept on it so the receipt stays theirs.

The full Personal Volume of a customer purchase counts in the referring Conductor's Sales Volume. The customer never qualifies, never holds a rank and never earns. In this version the referring Conductor receives no separate retail commission on the sale — the benefit is that the volume counts toward their own qualification, feeds their Commissionable Volume, and feeds their upline's Team Volume.

Worked, every step

M4 has no subscriptions at all this month. Two of M4's customers each subscribe to one support agent.

  1. QA agent $50.00, 50 PV. Secretary agent $50.00, 50 PV. Both book to M4's account.
  2. M4's Sales Volume is 100.00, which reaches the gate — M4 is qualified without having bought anything personally.
  3. M4's Commissionable Volume is 0.80 × 100.00 = 80.00.
  4. M4 earns nothing directly. M4's sponsor M1 earns 0.10 × 80.00 = 8.00 at level 1.
  5. Because M4 is now qualified, M4's leg counts as active for M1's rank, and M4 is eligible to be paid on anybody in M4's own downline.
Section ten · earning four

Instant Payout Approved, not built

Terms approved 2026-08-15. Still not built. It pays nothing today.

Both facts are true at once, and the second matters more. The terms below are settled policy: the rate, who is paid, what triggers it, the caps, and the rule that it does not roll up. But no software computes any of it — there is no table, no column and no function for Instant Payout anywhere in Orvanna, and not one has ever been paid. Nobody should tell a prospective Conductor that Instant Payout pays them.

Two questions are open, and each gates the build on its own: how a payout is recovered if the order behind it is refunded or charged back, for which no mechanism exists, and how these terms sit against the plan's 20 percent ceiling, which has no exception.

Every other payout in this plan resolves once, at the end of a calendar month. Instant Payout is the exception to that timing by design: it pays on the day, because the gap between doing the work and seeing the reward is what stops most new Conductors before they start.

Instant Payout breaks the rule the rest of this document taught you. Everywhere else a percentage sits on Commissionable Volume, which is 80 percent of the money. Instant Payout's 20 percent sits on the order price itself. On a $150.00 first order that is 0.20 × 150.00 = $30.00, not 0.20 × 120.00 = $24.00. A reader who assumes the usual basis will underestimate this mechanism by a quarter.

How the cap behaves. Had the first order been $400.00, the basis is capped at $250.00, so the Instant Payout is 0.20 × 250.00 = 50.00, the most any single Instant Payout can ever pay. The level 1 commission at month end is unaffected by the cap and is still calculated on the full order.

How this sits against the 20 percent ceiling — one of the two reasons it is not built. The ceiling is the plan's central promise and it has no exception: no order pays out more than 20 percent of its price. The terms above were approved separately and have never been reconciled with it. The worked example pays 42.00 on a 150.00 first order — 28 percent of the price against a 20 percent ceiling, eight points over. Nothing computes that today and nobody is paid it, and until the two are reconciled nobody will be. That question has to be settled, and written down, before any of this is built.

The rank ladder

Five rungs. Each one adds a level of reach, and nothing else. The percentages never change.

Rank Everything below must hold in the same month Reach

Leader Qualified, Team Volume 2,500.00 or more, and 3 or more active legs. 3 levels■■■□□
Director Qualified, Team Volume 10,000.00 or more, and 2 or more legs each containing a Builder or higher. 4 levels■■■■□
Executive Qualified, Team Volume 40,000.00 or more, and 2 or more legs each containing a Leader or higher. 5 levels■■■■■

"A leg containing a Builder" means anybody inside that leg, at any depth, held Builder or higher this month. A filled square is a level you are paid on; an open square is one you are not.

The rules that govern the ladder

  • Every requirement on a rung must hold in the same month. There is no mixing of a good January with a good March.
  • You hold the highest rung you meet. Nobody is stuck lower because of the order things are evaluated in.
  • Ranks recompute from scratch every month. There is no retention, no highest-ever-achieved title, and no grace period. You are what you did this month.
  • Every threshold is inclusive. Exactly 100.00 qualifies. Exactly 2,500.00 of Team Volume makes Leader.
  • Every rank above Member requires your own qualified month. A large team alone never carries a title.

The top two rungs are counted differently, on purpose. For Leader you count active legs, which asks whether your frontline is qualified. For Director you count legs that contain a Builder or higher anywhere inside; for Executive, legs containing a Leader or higher. That is what makes the top of this ladder about developing other leaders rather than accumulating volume.

Section thirteen

The calendar, rounding, and finality

How a month closes

  1. Every order is stamped with the month it counts toward at the moment it is created, in UTC. Subscription orders always stamp the first day of the month. The stamp never moves, so a late edit can never shift volume between months.
  2. Cutoff is the end of the calendar month. Nothing enters a closed month.
  3. The run computes volumes, qualification, ranks and every commission line for that month.
  4. Finalisation locks the run. A final run's statements cannot be edited, deleted, or quietly added to.
  5. A recomputation never edits history. It creates a new numbered run, and the old one stays on record marked superseded. Every statement traces to the exact run that produced it.

How the arithmetic is rounded

Rounding happens in exactly two places and nowhere else: once per Conductor per month, when Commissionable Volume is computed as 0.80 × Sales Volume; and once per commission line, when the rate is applied to the source Commissionable Volume.

Rounding is half up, so at exactly half a cent the Conductor gets the extra cent. Totals are never rounded twice. Your statement total is the sum of your already-rounded lines, and the company total is the sum of the already-rounded statements. That is what stops a statement from failing to add up.

With the current catalog rounding never actually fires: every price is a multiple of $50.00, so every Commissionable Volume is a multiple of 40, and every rate lands exactly on a cent.

Same data, same plan version, same answer. The organisation is photographed once at the start of a run and every later step reads that photograph, so editing the tree while a run is in flight cannot change that run's output. Every figure is fixed-point decimal, never floating point. Run the same month twice and it agrees to the cent.

Section fifteen

The Builder plan in one view

Three layers and two laws. Layer 1 is the plan in part one, unchanged. The two new layers pay for something part one does not pay for at all: turning a recruit into a leader. The two laws decide when that money stops and how much of it can ever exist.

Layer What it is Why it exists
Layer 1, the spine Unilevel version 1.3 exactly as it runs today: 10, 5, 5, 3 and 2 percent of the source Conductor’s Commissionable Volume across five levels, paid depth set by rank, one gate at 100.00 Sales Volume. Not a dial, not a parameter, not touched. Everything the current plan already earns: it is explainable, it is proven, and a Conductor who understands today’s plan already understands most of this one.
Layer 2, the Builder override Any month somebody in your organisation holds Builder rank, you earn a percentage of that Builder’s whole group volume, and the developer one generation further up earns a smaller percentage of the same thing. Two generations deep, no more. The team-building engine. Developing a Builder pays you on that Builder’s entire organisation; developing Builders who develop Builders pays a second generation.
Layer 3, the second-leg bonus A bonus on your second-strongest leg’s volume, with a small step-up as you hold more active legs. Balance. One giant leg pays nothing extra, by construction, so the rational move is a second real team, then a third.

The two laws

Law A · decay

Every layer recomputes from scratch each month, from that month's actual orders and that month's actual ranks. Nothing is banked, grandfathered or carried forward. A Builder who lapses stops paying their upline that month. What it protects: sustained engagement — helping your people stay active is worth money to you every month, and the plan prices it exactly.

Law B · the waterfall

Every source member-month contributes a fixed pool: 25 percent of its Commissionable Volume, which is 20 percent of its price. Layer 1 draws from that pool first, layer 2 second, layer 3 last. When the pool runs out, the junior layers share what is left in proportion. What it protects: solvency — no order can ever pay out more than 20 percent of its price, whatever the rates are set to, because the ceiling is the arithmetic the amounts are computed by, not a limit somebody watches afterward.

The rates as modelled

Builder override generation one, 1.5 percent of the Builder's group volume; generation two, 1.0 percent. Second-leg bonus, 2.0 percent of the second-strongest leg, stepping to 2.3 percent with three active legs and 2.5 percent with four or more. The pool that funds all of it is 25 percent of Commissionable Volume per order. These figures are the output of a single calibration pass, and every laboratory run records the exact rates it used.

Sections sixteen and seventeen

The two new layers

Layer two: the Builder override

Part one is passive about leadership: it pays you on whatever depth happens to exist below you, and nothing extra for the work of turning a recruit into somebody who can build. Layer 2 is the answer to that.

Your group volume is your own Commissionable Volume plus that of everybody in your entire downline, at every depth, with nothing excluded. Any month a Conductor in your organisation holds Builder rank or higher, that Builder's whole group volume becomes a basis. The qualified Conductor above them with no other Builder in between earns generation one, 1.5 percent of that group volume. The next qualified Conductor above that, with one Builder boundary crossed, earns generation two, 1.0 percent of the same group volume. There is no generation three.

No breakaway. In the plan family this mechanism comes from, promoting somebody removes their whole group from your volume — the plan punishes the promotion it says it wants, and the field learns to hold its best people down. No volume ever leaves anybody's group here, at any rank, ever. A nested Builder's group stays inside every group that encloses it. Developing a Builder is pure addition with zero subtraction anywhere.

How the two generations are found. Start at the Builder and walk up the sponsorship chain carrying a counter that starts at zero. At each Conductor: if they are qualified and the counter is zero and generation one is unassigned, they earn generation one; otherwise if they are qualified and the counter is one and generation two is unassigned, they earn generation two. Then, if that same Conductor holds Builder or higher, add one to the counter. Stop when the counter reaches two, or at the top of the tree.

  • Generation one usually goes to the Builder's own sponsor, which is the case everybody pictures.
  • If the sponsor is not qualified that month, the walk passes over them without counting them. An unqualified Conductor neither earns nor blocks.
  • One Conductor can collect generation one on one Builder and generation two on a deeper Builder in the same month. They are separate walks.
  • You do not have to be a Builder yourself to earn a Builder override — you have to be qualified, with a Builder below you. This is a recorded decision, not an oversight.

Stated honestly, because it is the cost of that choice: because groups nest, one order's volume can back override claims for several Conductors at once. Layer 2 is therefore not bounded by its own rates. It is bounded by Law B, which is where all the bounding in this plan happens.

Layer three: the second-leg bonus

A bonus on your second-strongest leg's volume, with a small step-up as you hold more active legs: 2.0 percent at two active legs, 2.3 at three, 2.5 at four or more. One giant leg pays nothing extra, by construction, so the rational move is a second real team, then a third.

The basis is a leg, which excludes you by construction — no Conductor is ever paid on their own volume as the source. And because the rate steps up while the basis is diluted, the two move against each other, which is what makes the mechanism resistant to being gamed. Section twenty-one shows what happened when a reviewer tried.

Sections eighteen and nineteen

The two laws, worked

Law A: nothing carries over

Every layer is recomputed from scratch each month, from that month's orders and that month's ranks. There is no bank, no grandfathering, no carry-forward and no grace period. The consequence people find uncomfortable is the honest one: a Builder you developed who lapses this month stops paying you this month, and last month's override does not protect you.

The consequence people underestimate is the other one: volume withheld is pay lost, permanently. There is nothing to bank it into, so there is never a reason to hold a month back.

Law B: the twenty percent promise, enforced

Each source member-month contributes a pool of 25 percent of its Commissionable Volume — 20 percent of its price. The spine draws first and always fits, because its five rates sum to exactly 25 percent. The override draws next. The bonus draws last. When the pool is exhausted, the junior layers share what remains in proportion to their claims, and any claim above the pool is recorded in full and paid at zero.

Why this is a promise rather than a policy. The ceiling is not a threshold somebody monitors and reacts to. It is the arithmetic the amounts are computed by: the pool is finite before any layer is evaluated, so exceeding it is not a state the engine can reach. Across every run made, including hostile fixtures built specifically to break it, zero Conductor-months exceeded 20 percent.

Section twenty

What the model measured

What this model can and cannot show. The laboratory replays orders that really happened, under different rules. It can show exactly how pay would be redistributed toward structure and how the ceiling behaves. It cannot show that a single Conductor would have behaved differently under those rules. Every statement below is a claim about the shape of the payout, not a measured behavioural response. Separately, the modelled population is generated with steady growth and almost no cancellation, so it cannot certify how volatile real checks would be.

What this plan does not do, said before anybody else says it

Section twenty-one

What we attacked, and what it paid back

Before this plan was shown to anybody, a reviewer was hired to break it, briefed as a veteran of this industry and told to attack it with everything the industry knows. Every attack below was run in the engine at the rates of record, not argued on paper. The numbers are what the engine paid.

Attack one: split your recruits across more legs to chase the rate step

The same six recruits, the same total of 700.00 Sales Volume, rearranged three ways.

The bonus falls from 4.80 to 3.68 to 2.00 as the rate step rises, because dilution beats the step at every count. Fixed downline volume V split across two equal legs pays 0.0100 × V, across three 0.0077 × V, across four 0.0063 × V. Two real balanced legs is the optimum, which is the design goal wearing its own name. Splitting also destroys overrides: the nested arrangement pays 7.20 of them, the split arrangements pay none.

The uncomfortable finding from the same test, published because a reviewer will find it anyway. The attacker's own best outcome is the flat, undeveloped shape — 50.00 — because the 10 percent frontline rate beats every development rate in the plan. The plan pays the field most for the developed shape, 79.20 against 50.00, and it pays the individual most for the flat one. This plan does not reverse a unilevel's preference for width among your own recruits, and it should never be pitched as though it does.

Attack two: starve your strongest leg so the second one looks bigger

Base month: legs of 240.00 and 120.00, bonus 2.40, total pay 38.40. Cut the strongest frontline's Sales Volume from 300.00 to 100.00 and the legs become 80.00 and 120.00 — the second-ranked value falls to 80.00, the bonus falls to 1.60, level pay falls from 36.00 to 20.00, and total pay falls from 38.40 to 21.60. Removing volume from any leg can never raise the second-largest value, and there is nothing to bank it into. Volume withheld is pay lost, permanently.

Sections twenty-two and twenty-three

The hard questions, and what is not settled

Part two carries the things that went wrong, the predictions that turned out false, and the one incentive inversion an outside reviewer found and we could not remove. Those are here on purpose. A plan document that answers the awkward question before it is asked is worth more than one that waits.

“Is this just another pyramid with software on top?”

Every commission line in both plans is computed from Commissionable Volume, which is 80 percent of product dollars. No line, rate or parameter anywhere pays on a count of recruits, enrollments or sign-ups — a full run of 1,001 Conductors was audited for exactly that, and found zero. You cannot be paid for enrolling somebody; you can only be paid when they buy something.

“Can I buy my way to the top?”

One Conductor with 10,000.00 of Sales Volume and no downline was run through the engine. Total earned: 0.00. Rank: Member. Your own purchases buy your qualification and nothing else. Builder requires two other people each reaching 100.00; Leader and above require Team Volume, which excludes your own.

“Does the plan pay more people than the one it replaces?”

No. 207 against 206, of 1,001. A stairstep breakaway tested in the same laboratory pays 448. Concentration is slightly higher, not lower: Gini 0.9687 against 0.9625. The honest sentence is the same paid population with a far deeper premium on development — nothing about breadth.

“What stops the payout drifting to 45 percent in three years?”

Law B. Each order contributes a finite pool of 25 percent of its Commissionable Volume before any layer is evaluated, so exceeding it is not a state the engine can reach. Measured drift is 1.12 points across five modelled month-steps, and it is bounded by arithmetic rather than by somebody watching a dashboard.

“If the incentives are so good, why does the flat shape pay the attacker more?”

Because the 10 percent frontline rate beats every development rate in the plan. It is the one inversion an outside reviewer found and we could not remove: the plan pays the field most for the developed shape, 79.20 against 50.00, and the individual most for the flat one. It is printed here rather than left to be discovered.

“Why is Instant Payout in a plan document if it pays nothing?”

Because its terms are approved policy and people are already describing it as though it works. Printing it with the words "not built" attached, twice, is the only way to keep that description honest. Two open questions gate the build — refund recovery, and its 28-percent-of-price worked example against a 20 percent ceiling with no exception.

What is not settled

  • Whether an override earner should have to hold rank themselves. Today the walk requires only that they are qualified with a Builder below them. Adding a rank test would gate the same behaviour twice; leaving it out pays some people who did less of the work.
  • Refunds, chargebacks and clawbacks. No rule exists for reversing volume or reclaiming paid commission, in either plan. The rule will be published before refunds exist.
  • How Instant Payout reconciles with the 20 percent ceiling. Unresolved, and one of the two conditions on it ever being built.
  • Compression. Named as the flagship change for a future version, and not modelled here.
  • A member-facing name for the Builder plan. It is identified today only by its engineering code, orvanna_builder. Naming it is a decision that has not been made.
  • Bundle, pack and one-time volume. The catalog sells them; version 1.3 recognises volume from the twelve individual agents only.

Nothing in part two takes effect unless and until this document says a version including it is effective from a stated month. Until then, part one is the plan, in full, and no Conductor has been paid one cent by either new layer.
