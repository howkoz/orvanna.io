The opportunity

What is a Conductor

A Conductor is what Orvanna calls the people who sell its artificial intelligence (AI) agents. You find the businesses around you that are drowning in work nobody enjoys, you work out which agents would take that work off them, and you stay on as the human who knows their setup.

The name is not decoration. A conductor does not play the instruments. A conductor decides which instruments the piece needs, gets them started together, and keeps them in time when the tempo changes. That is the job, almost exactly.

The name

Why Conductor and not distributor

Most direct selling companies borrow their word from a warehouse: distributor, dealer, representative. Those words fit when the thing being moved is a box. Orvanna does not ship boxes. Its products are agents that go to work inside somebody else's business, and the person who sold them is still needed on the day after the sale.

You do not play

You do not do the bookkeeping, chase the declined cards, or file the returns. The agents do that. Your hands stay off the instruments, which is what makes the work scale past your own hours.

You choose the score

A florist and a freight broker do not need the same agents. Picking the right two or three for a specific business, in the right order, is the part that takes judgment, and it is the part software cannot do for itself.

You keep time

Businesses change. The ensemble that fit a shop in March is short a player by September, when it starts selling into a second country and suddenly has filings it has never done before.

Vocabulary

Two words that must never be confused

Conductors use these two words constantly and they mean completely different things. Getting them mixed up in front of a business owner is the fastest way to sound like you do not understand what you are selling.

Ensemble

The agents

Your ensemble is the set of Orvanna agents running on an account, yours or a customer's. An ensemble is software. It is bought, it is priced in dollars, and every item carries Personal Volume (PV), the number that decides whether a month counts as qualified.

Team

The people

Your team, also called your organization, is the people you sponsored, the people they sponsored, and so on down. A team is people. Nobody in it is bought, nobody in it carries Personal Volume, and nothing is ever paid for adding a name to it.

Say it once and it stays straight: an ensemble plays, a team sells.

The actual product

You are not selling software

Nobody wakes up wanting to buy software. What people want is for a specific, hated, recurring job to stop being theirs. That is what is actually being sold here, and a Conductor who understands that outsells one who has memorized the catalog.

An illustration, not a customer story

A plumbing company with two vans

The owner quotes all day and invoices at night. Three invoices a month go unpaid and nobody chases them until the bank balance makes it urgent. Every quarter the tax return happens in a panic across a weekend, from a shoebox of receipts.

A weak Conductor opens the catalog and starts at Payment Agent. A good one asks which of those three things they would pay to never think about again, and listens. If the answer is the quarterly weekend, the answer is the Tax Agent, on its own, and nothing else this month. One agent. Set up in an afternoon. The next quarter ends with a prepared return waiting for their accountant instead of a shoebox.

That is the whole sale. The second and third agents come later, after the owner has watched the first one work and starts naming the next thing they hate.

Lead with their week

The opening question is what ate the most time last week, not which tier they want. If you cannot name the hour you are giving back, you do not have a sale yet.

Sell one thing first

A business with one loud problem does not need twelve agents. Overselling the first order is the most common way a Conductor loses the second one.

Be honest about the boundary

Orvanna agents propose and the owner approves. Nothing is filed, paid, published, or ordered without a human yes. Cautious owners buy because of that line, so never blur it to sound impressive.

The work

What a Conductor actually does

Five activities, and there is not a sixth. Everything a working Conductor does in a week is one of these.

1. Listen for the bottleneck

Conversations with owners you already have a reason to talk to, about their week rather than about Orvanna. You are looking for the recurring job that costs them evenings. Most of these conversations end without a sale, and that is the normal result, not a failure.

2. Fit the ensemble

Match the bottleneck to the smallest set of agents that fixes it. Read the product page for what an agent does day to day and check that it matches what you heard. If nothing fits, say so. Some problems are hiring problems, not agent problems.

3. Set it up and stay a week

Connect the agent to the tools it needs, agree the approval boundaries with the owner, and read the first outputs together. The first week is where an ensemble either becomes part of how they work or quietly gets ignored.

4. Check in when things change

A quarterly note, not a monthly pitch. New country, new season, new staff member, first employee: each of those changes which agents make sense. You are the person who already knows their setup, which is why the second sale is easier than the first.

5. Sponsor people who want to do the same

Sponsoring means somebody joins Orvanna underneath you and starts conducting their own ensembles and their own customers. You teach them the only way that works: you do one setup alongside them, then you watch them do the next one and say nothing. People you sponsor become your team, and their work is what opens the deeper levels of the compensation plan.

Getting started

The first ninety days

In order. Skipping ahead is the most common mistake, and the most expensive one is skipping the first step, because you cannot fit an ensemble you have never run.

  1. Week 1

    Run one agent yourself

    Pick the agent that fixes your own worst recurring hour, subscribe to it, and use it properly. Not a demonstration account. The real thing, on your own work, with your own approval boundaries set.

    Done when you have read one of its outputs and changed something you actually do because of it.

  2. Weeks 1 to 2

    Learn three agents properly

    Three, not twelve. Read each one's product page end to end, including the day to day section and where its authority stops. Being able to describe three agents precisely beats being able to list all of them.

    Done when you can explain each of the three, out loud, without the page in front of you.

  3. Week 2

    Write your list of twenty

    Twenty businesses you already have a reason to speak to: people you buy from, people you have worked with, people you know socially who run something. Next to each name, write the one job you suspect eats their week.

    Done when the list exists in writing and every name carries a guess.

  4. Weeks 2 to 6

    Have ten conversations

    Work the list. The conversation is about their week, and your job is to find out whether your guess was right. Do not present the catalog. Expect most of the ten to go nowhere, and keep notes on all of them, because a no this quarter is often a yes after their next busy season.

    Done when ten conversations have happened and at least one person has asked what it costs.

  5. Weeks 4 to 8

    Do your first setup, and stay through the first week

    One agent for one business. Get it connected, agree what it may do alone and what it must bring to the owner, and check in on days two, four and seven. This week is the entire difference between a customer who renews and one who forgets they subscribed.

    Done when the owner has approved something the agent proposed, without you in the room.

  6. Every calendar month

    Reach a qualified month

    One hundred Personal Volume (PV) booked to your account inside one calendar month makes that month qualified, and qualification is what makes you eligible to be paid at all. Volume comes from your own subscriptions or from customers who buy through you, in any combination. It resets on the first of every month.

    Done when your account shows 100 PV or more before the month ends.

  7. Weeks 8 to 12

    Sponsor your first Conductor

    Usually somebody who watched you do steps 4 and 5 and wanted the same thing. Teach by repetition, not by talking: do one setup beside them, then attend their next one silently. Then send them back to step 1, because the sequence works for them for the same reason it worked for you.

    Done when they have run a setup you did not speak during.

Earning

How a Conductor is paid

The mechanisms, in plain English. Every rate, every threshold and a full worked example live in the compensation brochure, which is the only document that governs. Nothing on this page is a promise of income, and no figures are quoted here on purpose: the brochure is the source of truth and a second copy of a number is just a chance for the two to disagree.

Mechanism one

Volume pays, not signatures

Nothing is ever paid for enrolling a person. What pays is what people buy: your own customers, the Conductors you sponsored, and the people they sponsored. Every product carries Personal Volume, a share of that volume is commissionable, and the commissionable part is what percentages are applied to.

Mechanism two

Rank buys reach, not rate

Advancing in rank does not increase what you earn on the people you sponsored directly. It opens levels further down your organization that you were not paid on before. This is worth understanding early, because it means rank is about helping the people under you stay active, never about buying your way up.

Mechanism three

Customers count toward your month

Someone can buy agents through you without joining Orvanna at all. That order books to your account, so its volume counts toward your qualified month. A Conductor's qualification can therefore rest on what they sold, not only on what they bought.

Before you decide

What this is not

Anyone who has sat through a direct selling presentation before is waiting for this section, and most decks do not have one. Here is ours.

  • No income is promised, implied, or described as typical. This page explains mechanisms. It contains no earnings figures, and you should be suspicious of any Conductor who quotes you one.
  • Buying more agents than you use is not a strategy. Your own purchases make your month qualified and they pay the people above you. They do not pay you. If an ensemble is not doing work you would otherwise pay a person to do, do not buy it.
  • Rank does not carry over. Every rank is recomputed from scratch each month. There is no highest ever title and no grace period.
  • An unqualified month pays nothing. Not reduced, not deferred, and no catch up payment later. However large your organization is, missing your own qualification in a month means no commission for that month.
  • Some problems are not agent problems. Part of the job is telling a business owner that what they need is a bookkeeper, a second van, or a hard conversation with a supplier. Say it. It is the reason they will call you next year.
  • The compensation brochure governs. If any sentence on this page disagrees with it, this page is the one that is wrong.

Fit

Is this you

It tends to suit people who

  • Already spend time around small business owners, in any trade.
  • Would rather explain one thing properly than pitch twelve.
  • Are patient enough to sit through somebody else's first week.
  • Are comfortable being the person who is called when something breaks.

It tends not to suit people who

  • Are looking for income that arrives without contact with customers.
  • Find it hard to say that something is not right for somebody.
  • Want to sell a product they have not personally used.
  • Need a predictable amount by a particular date.
