What this library is for

A Conductor who knows the product sells differently from one who knows only the pitch. The difference shows up in the second question a customer asks.

One set of facts

Every price and every Personal Volume (PV) figure in this library is read at load time from the same catalog file the shop reads. There is no second copy to fall out of step, here or on any agent page.

A page for every agent

Each of the sixteen has its own address you can share, bookmark, or print. Every page answers the same six questions in the same order, so once you can read one you can read all of them.

Honest about limits

Every agent has an approval boundary, and this library states it. An agent that prepares a tax return does not file it. An agent that drafts a refund does not issue it. Saying so builds more trust than hiding it.

You are a Conductor

Orvanna sells agents, and the person who leads them is a Conductor. The word was chosen because it does real work: an agent is capable on its own and considerably more useful when someone decides what plays, when, and together with what. That is the job.

Two words sit next to it and they must never be swapped, because a reader who confuses them once will stay confused. Your ensemble is your agents, the products you run. Your team, or your organisation, is the people you have sponsored. Agents are never a team; people are never an ensemble.

Two words you will use constantly

Personal Volume (PV) is the point value a purchase carries. At Orvanna the arithmetic is deliberately simple: PV equals dollars, in both billing modes. A subscription grants its PV every month it stays active; a one-time purchase grants its PV once. If you can read the price, you know the PV.

Sales Volume (SV) is all the PV booked to one account in one calendar month. Reach 100.00 SV in a month and that month is qualified. Qualification is a gate in the compensation plan, not a promise of any amount; the plan booklet is the only place that describes what follows from it.

The four tiers, and why each one exists

The tier label on every item is one of four words: domain, support, bundle, pack. The distinction is not decoration. Each tier answers a different question, and knowing which question a customer is asking is most of the sale.

Domain agent

A domain agent owns a whole business function, the kind that would otherwise be a department: payments, shipping, pricing, inventory, marketing, tax preparation. It has its own queue of work, its own daily rhythm, and its own approval boundary with the owner.

The test is simple. Could you draw a box on an organization chart around what this agent does, and would that box have a budget? If yes, it is a domain agent. It costs the higher of the two prices because it is the thing a business buys first and keeps longest.

Support agent

A support agent fills a staff role rather than a business function. It ships a fix, tests a change, keeps the calendar, balances the books, answers the inbox, or reads the whole picture back to you once a week.

On its own it is genuinely useful. It is not a department, and the price says so: exactly half. That is a deliberate piece of arithmetic. Two support agents equal one domain agent in both dollars and Personal Volume (PV), which matters at the qualification line.

Bundle

A bundle is a managed group. There is exactly one in the catalog: the Manager Agent, which contains the Software Engineer, Secretary, and Accounting agents.

What you buy on top of those three is the management layer itself: one point of contact, one shared task list, one daily summary instead of three. That is why it is the only item priced above the sum of its parts.

Pack

A pack is a curated formation: a set of agents chosen to cover a stage of business, with no management layer added unless the pack happens to contain the Manager Agent, which Constellation does.

Packs are priced at exactly the sum of their parts. No discount, no premium. What you are buying is the shortlist, and saying that plainly is more persuasive than implying a saving that is not there.

The one sentence that separates bundle from pack

A bundle sells you a manager, so it costs more than its contents. A pack sells you a shortlist, so it costs exactly its contents.

Say this, not the other thing

All three packs cost exactly what their contents cost. There is no saving, and a Conductor who implies one will be caught by the first customer who adds it up. What a pack buys is curation: somebody already decided which agents belong together at this stage, and you do not have to.

The Manager Agent runs the other way. Its three agents come to a month bought separately, and the bundle is a month. The difference is the coordination layer, which is the product. Selling it as a discount would be both false and weaker than the truth.

Why included agents show at $0.00

Put a pack in a cart and you will see the pack line at its full price, then one indented line per included agent at $0.00 with an Included tag. That zero is not a discount and not a fault. It is the statement that the parent line already carries the full price and the full Personal Volume (PV) for the whole group.

Two things follow. Money is never charged twice, and volume is never counted twice: the PV on a pack is the whole volume for that purchase, not the pack's PV plus the PV of everything inside it.

Pack, or the same agents separately?

Take the pack when the shortlist is doing work for you and you want the whole formation live on the same day. Take the agents individually when you expect to add or drop them one at a time, or when you want the flexibility to swap one out next month.

The price and the Personal Volume (PV) come out identical either way. That is the point: a pack is a convenience, never an arbitrage.

How to choose

Organized by the situation the buyer is actually in, not by the shape of the catalog. Find the sentence that sounds like them, then open the entry it points at.

"I am opening a storefront this month and I have never automated anything."

Start with the Ignition Pack at a month. Money in and accounted for, questions answered, commitments kept, and nothing else to learn yet.

If the budget is the constraint, the Payment Agent alone at a month is the single item that most often pays for itself, because worked declines are recovered revenue rather than saved effort.

"I ship physical goods and I find out about late parcels from angry customers."

Shipping Agent first, at a month. It is the one that changes who discovers the problem.

Add Customer Care at a month next, because the two together close the loop: shipping catches the delay and care tells the customer before they ask.

"My supplier costs moved and I honestly do not know which items still make money."

Pricing Agent, at a month. It recomputes margin per item and gives you a weekly review to approve line by line.

Pair it with Accounting at a month if your books are behind, because a margin calculation is only as good as the cost data underneath it.

"Cash is tied up in stock and I keep running out of the wrong things."

Inventory Agent, at a month. Nightly counts, velocity per item, reorder points that move with the numbers, and purchase orders drafted at the line.

It reports into Pricing and Accounting on its own, so those two get better the day you add it.

"We are growing and things are starting to break: promotions, prices, the site."

Momentum Pack, at a month. It staffs both sides of the seam growth tears open, so campaigns cannot outrun margin and changes cannot outrun testing.

Buy it when the growth is already visible. Bought in anticipation it is capacity waiting for work.

"My inbox is the bottleneck. I answer the same questions all week."

Customer Care, at a month. It answers what it can prove from your records, around the clock, and escalates the rest with a recommendation attached.

Add the Secretary at a month if the follow-ups you promise in those conversations are the thing that slips. Together they are , which is a qualified month.

"Filing dates keep arriving before I am ready for them."

Tax Agent, at a month, with Accounting at a month underneath it. The Tax Agent prepares and never files, so your accountant stays the authority and simply signs earlier.

Be clear about that boundary in the conversation. It is a feature, and hiding it invites a complaint later.

"I already run several agents and now I spend my time telling them what to do."

Manager Agent, at a month. One task list, one point of contact, one daily summary instead of three.

This is the one item that costs more than its parts, and that difference is exactly what the customer is asking to buy.

"I would be hiring across several functions at once if I could afford it."

Constellation Pack, at a month. All six domain agents plus the Manager Agent, which is ten agents once the Manager's own trio is counted.

The value here is the handoffs. Settlements reach the books, stock levels reach reorders, and costs reach prices without anyone carrying a spreadsheet between them.

"I just want to understand the smallest purchase that makes a month qualified."

A qualified month means Sales Volume (SV) of 100.00 or more, and exactly 100.00 qualifies. So it is one domain agent at , or any two support agents at each. A single support agent is : enrolled, not qualified.

Two things to say carefully. Qualification is a gate in the compensation plan, not a promise of any amount, and the plan booklet is the only place that describes what follows from it. And volume booked from what a customer buys through you counts the same as volume you buy yourself, so the smallest qualifying purchase is not necessarily a purchase you make.
