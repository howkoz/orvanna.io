Rules for card approvals on a call

  • Never ask the caller to read you a code from their bank. Not the six digits in a text message, not a code from a banking application, not ever. That request is the exact script of a real fraud technique, and it does not complete the approval anyway.
  • If the bank asks for an approval, only the cardholder can give it, on their own phone or computer. Send them a payment link and stay on the line.
  • A card you key here is a telephone order. Telephone orders sit outside Strong Customer Authentication (SCA), the European rule that requires these approvals, so the bank usually will not ask for one. The trade is that a fraud chargeback lands on us rather than on the card issuer.

Create the order, then send the caller a link. They pay on their own device, their bank can ask them to approve properly, and no card number is ever spoken aloud.

Not available on this sandbox yet: creating a hosted payment link needs a server function that has not been built, so nothing here can mint one. Until it exists, use the keyed telephone order path above and read the rules.
