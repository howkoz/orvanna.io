What is running, and what is only modelled

A compensation document is only worth reading if it is clear about which parts are real. This one is, in the first minute rather than the last.

Operating today

Part one, sections one to fourteen

Everything in part one of this brochure, from section one through the section headed "What version 1.3 does not do", which is section fourteen and the last section before the part two divider, is the plan the commission engine runs. It is unilevel version 1.3. Every monthly commission run this company has ever computed carries version 1.2 or version 1.3 of that specification, twelve runs across February to July 2026. Those runs produced statements. They are the plan.

Modelled, never paid

Part two, sections fifteen to twenty-four

Part two, everything from the section headed "The Builder plan in one view", which is section fifteen and carries the part two divider, through to the end of the glossary at section twenty-four, describes a two-layer extension called the Builder plan. It exists as a working implementation inside a compensation laboratory, where twenty-nine runs of it have been computed against recorded orders. It has never been the live plan, has never produced a statement, and has never paid one dollar to one person. Every figure in part two is a what-if computed from real recorded volume under different rules.

The important structural fact, and the reason part one is untouched. Layer 1 of the Builder plan is not a new version of the plan you are about to read. It IS the plan you are about to read, byte for byte: the same 10, 5, 5, 3 and 2 percent, the same five levels, the same rank-gated depth, the same 100.00 Sales Volume gate. That equality is a release test, not a claim: on the same month of orders, the Builder plan's level-pay lines and the live plan's lines were compared in both directions on earner, source, level, basis, rate, and amount, across 1,630 lines totalling 13,434.00, and there were zero differences. The two new layers are added on top and are paid out of money the live plan already leaves on the table. Nobody's existing pay is moved, reduced, or reshaped to fund them.

So read part one as documentation and part two as a proposal with its arithmetic and its bruises on display. Part two also carries the things that went wrong, the predictions that turned out false, and the one incentive inversion an outside reviewer found and we could not remove. Those are in there on purpose. A plan document that answers the awkward question before it is asked is worth more than one that waits.

Section one

How a Conductor earns, in one paragraph

A Conductor subscribes to one or more Orvanna agents, and every agent carries a Personal Volume (PV) figure equal to its price in dollars. Everything bought on that account in a calendar month adds up to their Sales Volume (SV), and 80 percent of that becomes Commissionable Volume (CV), which is the only number any commission is ever calculated from. Each month the company walks up the sponsorship tree from every buyer and pays a slice of that buyer's CV to the Conductors above them: 10 percent to the direct sponsor, then 5, 5, 3 and 2 percent to the next four up. Two gates decide whether a given Conductor actually receives their slice. First, they must be qualified, meaning their own SV that month was 100.00 or more. Second, the level must sit inside their paid depth, which their rank sets: a Member reaches one level down, a Builder two, a Leader three, a Director four, an Executive five. Anything that fails either gate is paid to nobody. That is the whole earning model: one mechanism, two gates, no bonus pools, no matching bonuses, no fast start, and no rebate on your own purchases.

Section two

Conductors, customers, and the two things called yours

Two roles exist in this plan, and one word does a lot of work, so it is worth spending a page on before any money moves.

Conductor

Somebody who has enrolled and holds a place in the sponsorship tree. A Conductor runs their own ensemble of agents, may sponsor other people, holds a rank each month, and can be paid commissions. Every person named in this brochure is a Conductor.

Customer

Somebody who subscribes to Orvanna agents through a referring Conductor without joining the tree. A customer pays the same prices and gets the same agents. A customer never holds a rank, never qualifies, and never earns. Their receipts always name them as the buyer.

The distinction that matters most in this document. Your ensemble is your agents, the software you subscribe to. Your team is your people, the Conductors you sponsored and everyone below them. The plan pays on both, but through completely different numbers: your ensemble drives your own volume and your qualification, while your team drives your Team Volume, your rank, and the commissions you receive. The two are never interchangeable, and this brochure never uses one word for the other.

One naming note, so nothing reads as a contradiction later. The lowest of the five ranks is titled Member. That is a rank name, not a second kind of person. Everybody in the plan is a Conductor; a Conductor who has not yet met a higher rung holds the rank of Member.

A subscription bills and grants its volume every month it stays active. Cancel it and the volume stops from the cancellation month forward; months already billed keep their volume.

The three volume numbers

  1. Personal Volume (PV) is printed on the product. The Payment Agent is 100 PV because it costs $100.00.
  2. Sales Volume (SV) is your month. Add the PV of everything booked to your account in one calendar month, your own subscriptions and your customers' subscriptions together.
  3. Commissionable Volume (CV) is 80 percent of your SV. It is the base for every percentage in this plan, without exception.

Read this twice, because it is the number one misreading of any plan of this kind.

Every percentage in this document is a percentage of Commissionable Volume, and Commissionable Volume is 80 percent of the money. So a 10 percent level 1 is not 10 percent of what the person spent. It is 10 percent of 80 percent, which is 8 percent of what they spent.

Worked in dollars: somebody you sponsored spends $150.00 this month. Their CV is 0.80 x 150.00 = 120.00. Your level 1 line is 0.10 x 120.00 = 12.00. As a fraction of their $150.00, that is 12.00 / 150.00 = 8 percent. The plan says this out loud here rather than letting anybody discover it on a statement later.

Why 80 and not 100

The 20 cents on every dollar that Commissionable Volume leaves behind never enters the commission calculation at all. It is the company's margin, decided before any percentage is applied. That is also why the plan can say truthfully that the ceiling on an order is 20 percent of the price: the five level percentages add to 25 percent of Commissionable Volume, and 25 percent of 80 percent is 20 percent.

That ceiling has no exception. It holds on every order, at every rank, in every shape of organisation, and every mechanism that pays anybody today is settled inside it.

Section five

The qualified month: the single gate

Before any money is paid to anybody, one test runs. It is the only qualification test in the plan, and the same number does two separate jobs.

Qualified means your own Sales Volume reached 100.00 in that calendar month. That is the whole test. Exactly 100.00 qualifies; every threshold in this plan is "or more", never "more than".

Job one: it decides whether you can be paid

Commissions for a month are paid only to Conductors who were qualified in that month. An unqualified month means no commission lines are created for you at all. Not reduced, not deferred, and never made up later.

Job two: it decides whether your leg counts

Rank requirements count active legs, and your leg is active for your sponsor only when you are qualified. Your sponsor's rank therefore depends on you staying active, which is the whole reason sponsors help.

Ways to reach 100

  • One domain agent at 100 PV.
  • Two support agents at 50 PV each.
  • Any mix of agents that reaches 100.
  • Customer subscriptions booked to your account, which count exactly the same as your own.
  • Any combination of the above.

Qualification can rest on what you sell, not only on what you buy. A Conductor with no subscriptions of their own and two customers each holding one support agent has 100 SV and is fully qualified. This matters beyond the arithmetic: plans where the only route to qualification is buying your own product are the ones regulators call pay to play. This is the structural answer to that, and it is the reason the customer role exists at all.

If you do not qualify in a month

  1. You are paid nothing that month.
  2. Your leg does not count as active for your sponsor's rank, however much volume sits below you.
  3. You cannot hold any rank above Member, even if your Team Volume and leg counts would otherwise reach Leader or Director.
  4. Your own volume still pays your upline in full. Losing qualification costs you, not the people above you.

Qualification resets every month. There are no grace months in this version; a qualified March does nothing for April.

Section six

One organisation, every example

Every worked example from here to the end of the brochure comes out of the same ten person organisation, so you never have to learn a new cast. It is drawn once, here. The labels M1 to M10 are the same labels the plan specification uses, so anything here can be checked against it line by line.

Earning one

Your frontline, at ten percent

This is where most of the money in the plan is made, because level 1 pays 10 percent, twice the rate of any other level.

The rule. You are paid 10 percent of the Commissionable Volume of every Conductor you personally sponsored, for as long as you are qualified that month. Their own qualification does not matter. If they bought, you are paid on it.

Worked, every step

Take M2 and M6 out of the organisation. M2 sponsored M6. This month M6 subscribed to the Marketing Agent and the Customer Care agent.

  1. Marketing Agent, $100.00, 100 PV. Customer Care agent, $50.00, 50 PV.
  2. M6's Sales Volume for the month is 100 + 50 = 150.00.
  3. M6's Commissionable Volume is 0.80 x 150.00 = 120.00.
  4. M2 is qualified this month, and level 1 sits inside every rank's paid depth, so both gates pass.
  5. M2's commission line is 0.10 x 120.00 = 12.00.

That is $12.00 on $150.00 of buying, which is 8 percent of what M6 actually spent. Not 10 percent, because the 10 percent applies to Commissionable Volume.

The same rule when the person below is not qualified

M8 sponsored M10. M10 subscribed to the Secretary agent only.

  1. M10's Sales Volume is 50.00, so M10's Commissionable Volume is 0.80 x 50.00 = 40.00.
  2. M10 is not qualified, because 50.00 is below 100.00. That does not matter here.
  3. M8 is qualified, so M8's level 1 line is 0.10 x 40.00 = 4.00.

M10 earns nothing that month and M10's leg does not count as active for M8's rank, but M10's volume still pays M8 in full. That asymmetry is deliberate and it is written into the plan: all Commissionable Volume pays upline, whoever generated it.

Worked, every step

M3 is a Builder, so M3's paid depth is 2. Underneath M3 sit M7 and M8 on the frontline, and M10 one level further down beneath M8.

  1. M7 subscribed to twelve domain agents and six support agents. Sales Volume = (12 x 100) + (6 x 50) = 1,500.00, so Commissionable Volume = 0.80 x 1,500.00 = 1,200.00.
  2. M8 subscribed to two support agents. Sales Volume = 100.00, Commissionable Volume = 80.00.
  3. M10 subscribed to one support agent. Sales Volume = 50.00, Commissionable Volume = 40.00.
  4. M7 is level 1 for M3: 0.10 x 1,200.00 = 120.00.
  5. M8 is level 1 for M3: 0.10 x 80.00 = 8.00.
  6. M10 is level 2 for M3: 0.05 x 40.00 = 2.00.
  7. M3's total is 120.00 + 8.00 + 2.00 = 130.00.

Now change one thing and nothing else. Suppose M3 held the rank of Member instead of Builder, so paid depth 1. M10 is out of reach. M3 earns 120.00 + 8.00 = 128.00, and the 2.00 is breakage. It is not pushed to the person above and it is not held for later. Nobody receives it.

The teaching point: rank buys reach, not rate. Going from Member to Builder does not increase what you earn on your frontline by a single cent. It opens a new row of people underneath them.

Earning three

Customer sales rolling up

Anybody can subscribe to Orvanna agents as a customer of a referring Conductor, without joining the tree. That order books onto the Conductor's account, tagged as a customer purchase with the customer's own identity kept on it so the receipt stays theirs.

The rule. The full Personal Volume of a customer purchase counts in the referring Conductor's Sales Volume. The customer never qualifies, never holds a rank, and never earns. In this version the referring Conductor does not receive a separate retail commission on the sale; the benefit is that the volume counts toward their own qualification, feeds their Commissionable Volume, and feeds their upline's Team Volume.

Worked, every step

M4 has no subscriptions at all this month. Two of M4's customers each subscribe to one support agent: one takes the Quality Assurance (QA) agent, the other takes the Secretary agent.

  1. QA agent $50.00, 50 PV. Secretary agent $50.00, 50 PV.
  2. Both orders book to M4's account, so M4's Sales Volume is 50 + 50 = 100.00.
  3. 100.00 reaches the gate, so M4 is qualified this month without having bought anything personally.
  4. M4's Commissionable Volume is 0.80 x 100.00 = 80.00.
  5. M4 earns nothing directly on these two sales. M4's sponsor, M1, earns 0.10 x 80.00 = 8.00 at level 1.
  6. Because M4 is now qualified, M4's leg counts as active for M1's rank, and M4 is eligible to be paid on anybody in M4's own downline.

Earning four

Instant Payout approved, not built

TERMS APPROVED 2026-08-15. STILL NOT BUILT. IT PAYS NOTHING TODAY.

These two facts are both true at once, and the second matters more than the first. The terms on this page are now settled policy: the rate, who is paid, what triggers it, the caps, and the rule that it does not roll up to the upline. But no software computes any of it. There is no table, no column and no function for Instant Payout anywhere in Orvanna. Not one has ever been paid.

An approved mechanism that does not exist yet is the one people start describing as though it works. Nobody should tell a prospective Conductor that Instant Payout pays them, because today it does not pay anybody. Two questions are still open, and each one on its own gates building this at all: how an Instant Payout is recovered if the order behind it is later refunded or charged back, for which there is no mechanism today, and how these terms sit against the plan's 20 percent ceiling, which has no exception. The second question is taken up at the end of this section.

Every other payout in this plan resolves once, at the end of a calendar month. Instant Payout is the exception to that timing by design: it pays on the day, because the gap between doing the work and seeing the reward is what stops most new Conductors before they start.

Read this sentence carefully, because Instant Payout breaks the rule the rest of this brochure taught you. Everywhere else, a percentage sits on Commissionable Volume, which is 80 percent of the money. Instant Payout is the one exception to that basis: its 20 percent sits on the order price itself. On a $150.00 first order that is 0.20 x 150.00 = $30.00, not 0.20 x 120.00 = $24.00. A reader who assumes the usual basis will underestimate this mechanism by a quarter.

Worked, every step

You sponsor somebody. Nineteen days after they enrol, they place their first order, a $150.00 subscription. You have bought agents of your own before, and this is your first Instant Payout this month.

  1. The order price is $150.00, which is below the $250.00 basis cap, so the whole price counts.
  2. Instant Payout = 0.20 x 150.00 = 30.00, paid to you the day the payment succeeds.
  3. That 30.00 stops with you. Your own sponsor is paid nothing on it, and neither is anybody above them. An Instant Payout creates no level pay, no depth pay and no volume, so there is nothing for it to travel up. If you were the fifth person in a chain of Conductors, the other four would see nothing from this payment.
  4. At month end the ordinary plan still runs. Their Commissionable Volume is 0.80 x 150.00 = 120.00. That volume does travel up the tree in the ordinary way, exactly as sections seven and eight described, because it came from the order and not from the Instant Payout.
  5. Your level 1 line is 0.10 x 120.00 = 12.00, exactly as it would have been without Instant Payout.
  6. Total to you on that first order: 30.00 + 12.00 = 42.00.

How the cap behaves

Suppose the first order had been $400.00 instead. The basis is capped at $250.00, so the Instant Payout is 0.20 x 250.00 = 50.00, which is the most any single Instant Payout can ever pay. The level 1 commission at month end is unaffected by the cap and is still calculated on the full order.

How this sits against the 20 percent ceiling, which is one of the two reasons it is not built. The ceiling in section four is the plan's central promise and it has no exception: no order pays out more than 20 percent of its price. The terms above were approved separately and have never been reconciled with that promise. The gap is worth printing rather than leaving for a reader to find. The worked example above pays 42.00 on a 150.00 first order, which is 28 percent of the price against a 20 percent ceiling, eight points over. Nothing computes that today and nobody is paid it, and until the two are reconciled nobody will be. That question has to be settled, and written down, before any of this is built. It is the second open gate on the build, alongside the refund and chargeback recovery named at the top of this section.

Five rungs. Each one adds a level of reach, and nothing else. The percentages never change.

The rules that govern the ladder

  • Every requirement on a rung must hold in the same month. There is no mixing of a good January with a good March.
  • You hold the highest rung you meet. Nobody is stuck on a lower rung because of the order things are evaluated in.
  • Ranks recompute from scratch every month. There is no retention, no highest ever achieved title, and no grace period. You are what you did this month.
  • Every threshold is inclusive. Exactly 100.00 qualifies. Exactly 2,500.00 of Team Volume makes Leader.
  • Every rank above Member requires your own qualified month. A large team alone never carries a title.

The top two rungs are counted differently, on purpose. For Leader you count active legs, which asks whether your frontline is qualified. For Director you count legs that contain a Builder or higher anywhere inside, and for Executive, legs that contain a Leader or higher. That is what makes the top of this ladder about developing other leaders rather than about accumulating volume.

Section thirteen

The calendar, rounding, and finality

How a month closes

  1. Every order is stamped with the month it counts toward at the moment it is created, in Coordinated Universal Time (UTC). Subscription orders always stamp the first day of the month. The stamp never moves afterward, so a late edit can never shift volume between months.
  2. Cutoff is the end of the calendar month. Nothing enters a closed month.
  3. The run computes volumes, qualification, ranks, and every commission line for that month.
  4. Finalisation locks the run. A final run's statements cannot be edited, deleted, or quietly added to.
  5. A recomputation never edits history. It creates a new numbered run, and the old one stays on record marked superseded. Every statement traces to the exact run that produced it.

How the arithmetic is rounded

Rounding happens in exactly two places and nowhere else:

  1. Once per Conductor per month, when Commissionable Volume is computed as 0.80 x Sales Volume.
  2. Once per commission line, when the rate is applied to the source Commissionable Volume.

Rounding is half up, so at exactly half a cent the Conductor gets the extra cent. Totals are never rounded twice. Your statement total is the sum of your already rounded lines, and the company total is the sum of the already rounded statements. That is what stops a statement from failing to add up.

With the current catalog, rounding never actually fires: every price is a multiple of $50.00, so every Commissionable Volume is a multiple of 40, and every rate lands exactly on a cent.

Same data, same plan version, same answer. The organisation is photographed once at the start of a run and every later step reads that photograph, so editing the tree while a run is in flight cannot change that run's output. Every figure is fixed point decimal, never floating point. Run the same month twice and it agrees to the cent.

Section sixteen

Layer two: the Builder override

The plan in part one is passive about leadership. It pays you on whatever depth happens to exist below you, and it pays you nothing extra for the work of turning a recruit into somebody who can build. Layer 2 is the answer to that, and it is the oldest mechanism in this industry.

What gets paid

Your group volume is your own Commissionable Volume plus the Commissionable Volume of everybody in your entire downline, at every depth, with nothing excluded.

Any month a Conductor in your organisation holds Builder rank or higher, that Builder's whole group volume becomes a basis. The qualified Conductor above them with no other Builder in between earns generation one, 1.5 percent of that group volume. The next qualified Conductor above that, with one Builder boundary crossed, earns generation two, 1.0 percent of the same group volume. There is no generation three.

The rule that makes this different from the classic version

In the plan family this mechanism comes from, promoting somebody removes their whole group from your volume. The plan punishes the promotion it says it wants, and the field learns to hold its best people down. That is called a breakaway, and this plan does not have one. No volume ever leaves anybody's group, at any rank, ever. A nested Builder's group stays inside every group that encloses it. Developing a Builder is pure addition to your check with zero subtraction anywhere, and there is no rank a Conductor below you can reach that costs you a cent of volume.

Stated honestly, because it is the cost of that choice: because groups nest, one order's volume can back override claims for several Conductors at once. Layer 2 is therefore not bounded by its own rates. It is bounded by Law B, in the section on Law B, which is where all the bounding in this plan happens.

How the two generations are found: the walk

Start at the Builder. Walk up the sponsorship chain toward the top, carrying a counter that starts at zero. At each Conductor you meet, first check whether they earn, then check whether they are a boundary.

  1. If they are qualified and the counter is zero and generation one is not yet assigned, they earn generation one.
  2. Otherwise, if they are qualified and the counter is one and generation two is not yet assigned, they earn generation two.
  3. Then, if that same Conductor holds Builder rank or higher, add one to the counter.
  4. Stop when the counter reaches two, or at the top of the tree.

Three consequences worth stating:

  • Generation one usually goes to the Builder's own sponsor, which is the case everybody pictures.
  • If the sponsor is not qualified that month, the walk passes over them without counting them, and generation one goes further up. An unqualified Conductor neither earns nor blocks.
  • One Conductor can collect generation one on one Builder and generation two on a deeper Builder in the same month. They are separate walks.

A note on who can earn it. You do not have to be a Builder yourself to earn a Builder override. You have to be qualified, and there has to be a Builder below you. The walk already demands that somebody did the development work; adding a rank test on the earner would gate the same behavior twice. This is a decision, recorded as an open question in the section on what is not settled, not an oversight.

Section seventeen

Layer three: the second-leg bonus

The most effective known way to make a compensation plan reward teamwork is to pay people on their weaker side rather than their stronger one. It is why binary plans dominate the fast-growth end of this industry. It also brings the two worst gaming problems in the industry with it: strategic placement in an artificial tree, and volume banked and carried forward. Layer 3 takes the engine and leaves both problems behind, because there is no artificial tree in this plan to place anybody in, and nothing carries forward.

What gets paid

A leg is one Conductor you personally sponsored plus that person's entire downline. A leg's volume is the Commissionable Volume of everybody in it, whether they are qualified or not, because volume is volume. A leg is active when the Conductor at its head is qualified this month, which is the same definition part one already uses.

Rank your legs by volume, highest first. If you are qualified and at least two of your legs are active, you earn a bonus on the volume of your SECOND-strongest leg. The rate is 2.0 percent with two active legs, 2.3 percent with three, and 2.5 percent with four or more.

One giant leg pays nothing extra, and that is the point. With one leg there is no second leg, so there is no basis and no bonus. With two legs of wildly different sizes, the bonus is computed on the smaller one. The plan will not pay you more for making one leg enormous. It will pay you for building a second real team, and then a third. That is the entire design intent of this layer, said plainly.

Two details a careful reader will ask about. Legs are ranked by volume across ALL of your legs, while eligibility and the rate step count only ACTIVE legs. So a Conductor with two active legs and a third, fat, inactive leg is paid on whichever leg is second by volume. Ties are broken by the identifier of the Conductor at the head of the leg, so the answer is the same every time the month is computed.

The rate steps are deliberately shallow. Large steps at magic numbers are what produce end-of-month buying to cross a threshold, and the section on what we attacked shows the arithmetic of why these steps are never worth buying: splitting volume across more legs loses more to dilution than the step gives back, at every count.

Section eighteen

Law A: nothing carries over

Every layer recomputes from scratch each month, from that month's orders and that month's ranks. There is no banking, no carryover, no highest-ever title, no grace month. The Builder set is recomputed monthly, so the override walk's boundaries are recomputed monthly. Legs are re-ranked monthly, so the second-strongest leg and the rate step are recomputed monthly. If a Builder below you lapses, your override on them stops that month.

What that is worth, exactly. Two exhibits, side by side, both required, in that order.

Exhibit one, the acceptance fixture: the loss is exactly the override, to the cent

Section nineteen

Law B: the twenty percent promise

Part one of this brochure already makes a promise: the most any order can ever pay is 20 percent of its price. Layers 2 and 3 do not raise that ceiling by one cent. They spend the head-room under it that the current plan leaves unspent. All the team-building money in this plan is reclaimed breakage, and nothing else.

How it works

The unit, defined once, because it is not the unit a reader expects. The ceiling is computed per source member-month: one Conductor's whole month of orders, taken together, counts as one unit. The modelled March holds 1,001 of those units, of which 641 have any volume at all. That same March holds 1,462 separate orders from those 641 buying Conductors, and 357 of them, 56 percent, placed more than one order. So a count of 1,001 is a count of Conductor-months and never a count of orders, and this document does not use the two words for one thing.

What the promise means exactly. The ceiling binds across a Conductor's whole month of orders taken together, not on each order separately. Across that month it holds exactly, and zero of the 1,001 modelled Conductor-months exceeded it. Inside a month that binds, one order can draw more of the pool than another, and 1,178 of March's orders, 81 percent, sit in months carrying more than one order.

Every source member-month contributes a pool equal to 25 percent of its Commissionable Volume. Since Commissionable Volume is 80 percent of the price, and 0.25 x 0.80 = 0.20, that pool is exactly 20 percent of the money that came in the door: the same ceiling the plan already publishes.

Then the layers draw from that pool in order of seniority.

  1. Layer 1 draws first and is always paid in full. The five level rates add up to 10 + 5 + 5 + 3 + 2 = 25 percent of Commissionable Volume, and there is at most one Conductor at each level, so layer 1 can never claim more than the pool holds. The spine always fits. It is never reduced. That is what makes "layer 1 unchanged" a fact rather than an intention.
  2. Layer 2 draws from what is left. If the override claims on that order fit, they are paid in full. If they do not, every override claim on that order is scaled down by the same factor.
  3. Layer 3 draws from what is left after that, by the same rule.

The factors are computed as closed formulas over the complete set of claims on each order, never by draining a ledger in some order, so no sequence of members or claims or layers can change any answer. Same orders in, same cents out, every time.

  • 35 of the 207 paid Conductors, about 17 percent, have at least one reduced line.
  • The total reduced away is 943.68, being 638.00 from layer 2 and 305.68 from layer 3, against 17,417.12 paid: about 5.4 percent of the run.
  • The worst-affected Conductor loses 324.14.   • Every cent of it comes off layers 2 and 3. The spine is structurally untouchable, so no Conductor's live-plan-equivalent pay is ever reduced by this.

The honest phrase is that proration is routine and junior: a working feature, not an edge case. An earlier draft of this plan predicted it would be rare. The measurement retired the word "rare" rather than defending it.

The statement rule. This is a commitment, not a nicety. A reduced line is not recomputable by the person receiving it. It depends on other people's claims against shared orders, order by order, at six decimal places. One real example: a modelled Conductor's bonus line shows a rate of 2.0 percent and a basis of 120.00. Rate times basis is 2.40. So the plan requires, as a rule of the plan and not as a feature of some internal tool: every reduced line on any Conductor's statement must print the amount claimed, the amount paid, and a one-line reason ("this order reached its 20 percent cap, and the two bonus layers shared what remained"). The engine already keeps the claimed and paid figures per line. A statement that shows a shrunken number without showing the claim and the why is not acceptable, and any build of a member-facing screen without it fails its release test.

Where the drift comes from also matters. It comes out of the gap between the 14.6 percent the live plan actually pays and the 25 point ceiling the price already carries. What a maturing field shrinks is the company's unpaid remainder, never the margin the product was priced against.

The uncomfortable finding from the same test, published because a reviewer will find it anyway. The attacker's own best outcome is the flat, undeveloped shape, 50.00, because the 10 percent frontline rate beats every development rate in the plan. The plan pays the FIELD most for the developed shape, 79.20 against 50.00, and it pays the individual most for the flat one. This plan does not reverse a unilevel's preference for width among your own recruits, and it should never be pitched as though it does.

Attack two: starve your strongest leg so the second one looks bigger

Removing volume from any leg can never raise the second-largest value, and there is nothing to bank it into, because nothing carries over. Volume withheld is pay lost, permanently.

The floor under that is structural, and it has to be stated precisely, because the general form of it is false. For commission generated BY the self-funded volume itself, no plan funded under a 20 percent ceiling can return more than 20 cents on a self-funded dollar, so a self-funding attack of that kind loses at least 80 percent, and this one loses 93.

What that bound does not cover, stated here rather than left to be found. It does not cover self-funded volume bought to UNLOCK pay on other people's volume, and unlocking is exactly what the 100.00 qualification gate does: a Conductor's own 100.00 purchase is what makes them eligible to collect level pay on their team. A Conductor who spends 100.00 to unlock pay on a large team is not bounded at 20 cents on their own dollar, because most of what they collect is generated by somebody else's volume.

Nor does the bound cover the approved Instant Payout terms, and that is a fact about the bound rather than about the ceiling. The Instant Payout section works a self-funded first order of 150.00 returning 42.00, which is 28 cents on the dollar, above the 20 this bound implies. The 20-cent figure is a bound on one channel. It is not a law of the plan, and an earlier draft of this page stated it as one.

Funding an artificial second leg to reach the bonus is the same trade: $100.00 a month of real subscription to return at most 0.025 x 80.00 = 2.00, a 98 percent monthly loss.

Attack four: buy rank and income with your own money

One Conductor, Sales Volume 10,000.00, no downline. Total earned: 0.00. Rank: Member.

Your own purchases buy your qualification, which is the industry-standard low gate, and they buy nothing else. Builder requires two ACTIVE legs, which means two other people each reaching 100.00 Sales Volume in that month. Leader and above require Team Volume, which excludes your own. And a large one-time purchase cannot spike a month: a $2,000.00 one-time order is recognised as 200 Personal Volume a month for ten months, not 2,000 at once, so it cannot be used to grab a rank for an override month.

What none of the reason codes can do

A separate check on the engine's output rather than its prose. In a full run of 1,001 Conductors:

  • Exactly four kinds of commission line exist, and every one of them is computed from Commissionable Volume, which is 80 percent of product dollars. No line, no rate, and no parameter anywhere in this plan pays on a count of recruits, enrollments, or sign-ups.
  • Zero lines pay a Conductor on their own volume as the source. Overrides pay the upline on a Builder's group. The bonus basis is a leg, which excludes you by construction.
  • Zero lines have a missing or non-positive basis.
  • Zero Conductor-months exceeded the 20 percent ceiling, in every run made, including every hostile fixture built specifically to break it.

What survives after every attack fails is the behavior the plan was built for: qualify yourself, keep two or more real legs active, develop Builders, and help them stay qualified, because Law A re-prices all of it every month. The best exploit anybody found is doing the work.

Section twenty-two

The hard questions, answered with numbers

The objections a professional will raise, each answered inside this document with figures rather than assurances. The reader is assumed unfriendly and numerate.

Overrides on group volume stack across generations and across nested Builders. The budget blows up.

The claims stack without limit. The PAYMENT cannot. Take an order carrying 80.00 of Commissionable Volume, so a pool of 20.00, sitting under ten nested Builders, so the override claims against it total two and a half times its entire pool. If a full five levels of qualified upline sit above it, level pay takes the whole 20.00, the remaining pool is zero, and every one of those override claims pays 0.00. If only two levels of qualified upline exist, level pay takes 12.00, the remaining 8.00 is shared across 48.00 of claims at a factor of 0.166666, and the ten Builders collectively receive exactly 8.00. In both cases the order pays out at most 20.00, to the cent.

In the actual modelled census the worst real order carries override claims at 0.56 of its pool, and zero of the 1,001 modelled Conductor-months paid over pool.

Paying on the weaker leg makes people sandbag the strong one or split volume artificially.

Both are worked with engine numbers in the section on what we attacked. Sandbagging loses: total pay falls 38.40 to 21.60. Splitting loses: the bonus falls 4.80 to 3.68 to 2.00 as legs are added, and overrides are forfeited entirely. There is nothing to bank volume into and no placement tree to park it in. Directing genuine new recruits into the weaker leg IS profitable, and that is the intended behavior, paid at the designed rate under the ceiling.

One qualifier this answer carries: that is the RANKING channel. The waterfall channel has the passive inversion disclosed in the section on Law A, which is bounded and watched there.

Monthly decay churns leaders who have one bad month.

Stated plainly: this version has no grace month, and the trade-off is real. Three things bound the damage.

First, the qualification bar is low and wide. 100.00 Sales Volume is one domain agent, and customer volume counts toward it, so a working leader's qualification rarely turns on a single order of their own.

Second, the loss lands FIRST on the one or two Builder generations nearest the lapse, and those are exactly the people positioned and now paid to intervene. The plan does not punish the bad month. It prices, for the sponsor, what preventing it was worth, to the cent. Above a binding ceiling the sign inverts, and that is disclosed in full in the section on Law A rather than left for a reader to find.

Third, it is measured. Decay events and their summed effect are counted every run, so the churn cost is an observed number before this plan is presented, not a hope. Across 215 modelled member-month pairs there were 22 declines, four to seven Conductors a month with falling override income, two drops of more than half, and one total wipe, which is the exhibit shown in the section on Law A. And the caveat rides along: the modelled population grows steadily and almost never cancels, so those six months certify only that decay is attributable and countable, never that real churn would look like that.

If observed decay proves too sharp, a one-month grace for Builder status is a single parameter, and adding it would be an amendment rather than a redesign. That decision is deliberately still open, and the section on what is not settled says so.

This double-pays the same volume.

Every override plan in this industry pays several people on one order. The honest question is whether the sum is bounded. The section on Law B follows one order all the way: three layers, four payments, 14.80 paid of a 20.00 ceiling, 5.20 unspent. The plan never pays more than the 20 percent of revenue the company already promises today. It pays the same ceiling to more purposes.

The ceiling will reduce the field's checks and they will be furious.

An earlier draft predicted proration would be rare and junior. The measurement kept the word junior and retired the word rare: 154 of the 641 Conductor-months that carry any volume are reduced on at least one layer, 24 percent, roughly one in four, and 35 of the 207 paid Conductors carry at least one reduced line. Those numbers, refreshed every run, are carried in any presentation of this plan.

What makes it survivable is structural and stated in the section on Law B: the spine is never reduced, so nobody's live-plan-equivalent pay ever falls; and every reduced line must print what it claimed, what it paid, and why. A shrunken number a member cannot recompute is survivable only when the statement itself shows the claim, the payment, and the reason.

Your payout ratio will creep as the field professionalises. You have built cost drift into the design, and it compresses margin exactly when you scale.

True, and answered structurally rather than denied. The drift is real, it is intended, it is plotted in the section on what the model measured at 0.22 points of Commissionable Volume a month at this maturity, and it is bounded above at 25 percent of Commissionable Volume per order by the same arithmetic that computes the payments. Every point of drift comes out of the gap between the 14.6 percent the plan pays today and the ceiling the price already carries. What maturity shrinks is the company's unpaid remainder, never the priced margin.

Section twenty-four

Key terms and definitions

The full list, in alphabetical order, including every term used anywhere in this brochure.

Active leg
A leg whose frontline Conductor is qualified this month. A leg with a large downline and an unqualified frontline Conductor is not active.
Breakage
Commission that the level percentages describe but nobody receives, because the person who would have received it was not qualified or the level was outside their paid depth.
Builder override
A payment to a qualified Conductor above somebody who holds Builder rank this month, computed on that Builder's entire group volume. Part two of this brochure only. Modelled, not operating.
Cap, or ceiling
The most one order can ever pay in commission: 25 percent of its Commissionable Volume, which is 20 percent of its price. Enforced inside the calculation, order by order.
Commissionable Volume (CV)
80 percent of Sales Volume, rounded to two decimals. The only base any commission percentage is ever applied to.
Commission run
One numbered, versioned batch calculation for one month. It produces every statement and is locked once it is marked final.
Compression
Skipping over an unqualified Conductor so the next qualified person moves into their slot. This version of the plan does not do it.
Conductor
Somebody who has enrolled and holds a place in the sponsorship tree. Conductors run an ensemble of agents, may sponsor other Conductors, hold a rank each month, and can be paid.
Customer
Somebody who subscribes through a referring Conductor without joining the tree. A customer never qualifies, never holds a rank, and never earns.
Decay
The rule that every layer recomputes from scratch each month from that month's orders and ranks, with nothing banked, carried forward, or grandfathered.
Downline
Everybody below you in the tree, at any depth.
Ensemble
The set of Orvanna agents a Conductor subscribes to. The ensemble drives that Conductor's own volume. It is never the word for their people.
Frontline
The Conductors you personally sponsored, and only those.
Generation
In the Builder override, a step counted in Builders rather than in levels. Generation one is the first qualified Conductor above a Builder with no other Builder in between. Generation two is the next one up, past one Builder boundary.
Group volume
A Conductor's own Commissionable Volume plus that of everybody in their entire downline at every depth. Groups nest: nothing ever leaves a group when somebody inside it ranks up.
Leg
One frontline Conductor together with that person's entire downline, treated as one branch.
Level
Plain distance down the tree from you to somebody in your downline. Your frontline is level 1, their frontline is level 2, and so on. Levels never skip in this version.
Paid depth
How many levels of your downline you are paid on, set by your rank: Member 1, Builder 2, Leader 3, Director 4, Executive 5.
Personal Volume (PV)
The volume figure carried by a product, which at Orvanna always equals its price in dollars.
Pool
The money one order makes available for commission: 25 percent of its Commissionable Volume.
Proration
Scaling every claim of one layer against one order down by the same factor, when those claims are larger than the pool that order has left. Level pay is never prorated.
Qualified month
A month in which your own Sales Volume reached 100.00 or more. The single gate for being paid and for your leg counting as active.
Rank
The title you hold for one month, recomputed from scratch each month, which sets your paid depth. The five ranks are Member, Builder, Leader, Director and Executive.
Sales Volume (SV)
The total Personal Volume booked to your account in one calendar month, whether you bought it or one of your customers did.
Second-leg bonus
A bonus on the volume of a Conductor's second-strongest leg, paid only when at least two legs are active. Part two of this brochure only. Modelled, not operating.
Spine
The name part two gives to layer 1, which is the live plan of part one, unchanged.
Sponsor
The one Conductor directly above you, the person who enrolled you.
Structure-linked share
The fraction of the total payout that comes from the Builder override and the second-leg bonus rather than from level pay. Never quoted without the participation counts beside it.
Team
The Conductors you sponsored and everybody below them. The team drives Team Volume and rank. It is never the word for your agents.
Team Volume (TV)
The Sales Volume of everybody strictly below you, added across your whole downline at every depth. It never includes your own Sales Volume.
Upline
Everybody above you: your sponsor, their sponsor, and so on to the top of the tree.
Volume month
The calendar month an order counts toward, stamped when the order is created and never moved afterward.
Waterfall
The order in which the layers draw from an order's pool: level pay first, overrides second, the second-leg bonus last.
