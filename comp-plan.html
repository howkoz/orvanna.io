Before you start

How to use this brochure

This document is built to be read like a short book, from the first page to the last. It starts with the words, then the volume, then the one gate that decides whether anybody gets paid at all, and only then the money. Read it in that order once and the rest of it stops needing explanation.

Every example shows its arithmetic. Nothing is rounded off behind the scenes and nothing is described in a percentage without also being shown in dollars. If a number here disagrees with a number anywhere else, the compensation plan booklet is the document that governs.

Acronym key for this page: Personal Volume (PV), Sales Volume (SV), Commissionable Volume (CV), Team Volume (TV), artificial intelligence (AI), Quality Assurance (QA), Coordinated Universal Time (UTC).

Demonstration document. Orvanna is a demonstration company and its Conductor program is a working prototype running on synthetic data. Every figure in this brochure is real to the demonstration's commission engine and to nothing else. No actual earnings, accounts, or payments exist.

Not an income claim. The worked examples exist to show how the arithmetic runs. They are illustrations, not projections, and nothing here promises or implies that any person would earn any amount.

Section one

How a Conductor earns, in one paragraph

A Conductor subscribes to one or more Orvanna agents, and every agent carries a Personal Volume (PV) figure equal to its price in dollars. Everything bought on that account in a calendar month adds up to their Sales Volume (SV), and 80 percent of that becomes Commissionable Volume (CV), which is the only number any commission is ever calculated from. Each month the company walks up the sponsorship tree from every buyer and pays a slice of that buyer's CV to the Conductors above them: 10 percent to the direct sponsor, then 5, 5, 3 and 2 percent to the next four up. Two gates decide whether a given Conductor actually receives their slice. First, they must be qualified, meaning their own SV that month was 100.00 or more. Second, the level must sit inside their paid depth, which their rank sets: a Member reaches one level down, a Builder two, a Leader three, a Director four, an Executive five. Anything that fails either gate is paid to nobody. That is the whole earning model: one mechanism, two gates, no bonus pools, no matching bonuses, no fast start, and no rebate on your own purchases.

Section two

Conductors, customers, and the two things called yours

Two roles exist in this plan, and one word does a lot of work, so it is worth spending a page on before any money moves.

Conductor

Somebody who has enrolled and holds a place in the sponsorship tree. A Conductor runs their own ensemble of agents, may sponsor other people, holds a rank each month, and can be paid commissions. Every person named in this brochure is a Conductor.

Customer

Somebody who subscribes to Orvanna agents through a referring Conductor without joining the tree. A customer pays the same prices and gets the same agents. A customer never holds a rank, never qualifies, and never earns. Their receipts always name them as the buyer.

The distinction that matters most in this document. Your ensemble is your agents, the software you subscribe to. Your team is your people, the Conductors you sponsored and everyone below them. The plan pays on both, but through completely different numbers: your ensemble drives your own volume and your qualification, while your team drives your Team Volume, your rank, and the commissions you receive. The two are never interchangeable, and this brochure never uses one word for the other.

One naming note, so nothing reads as a contradiction later. The lowest of the five ranks is titled Member. That is a rank name, not a second kind of person. Everybody in the plan is a Conductor; a Conductor who has not yet met a higher rung holds the rank of Member.

Section three

Every word, defined before it is used

One sentence each, with no jargon inside the definitions. The full glossary is at the back of the brochure; this short list is the working set.

Sponsor
The one Conductor directly above you, the person who enrolled you.
Upline
Everybody above you: your sponsor, their sponsor, and so on to the top of the tree.
Downline
Everybody below you, at any depth, however far it goes.
Frontline
The Conductors you personally sponsored, and only those.
Leg
One frontline Conductor plus that person's entire downline, treated as a single branch.
Level
Plain distance down the tree from you: your frontline is level 1, their frontline is level 2, and so on.
Personal Volume (PV)
The volume figure printed on a product, which at Orvanna always equals its price in dollars.
Sales Volume (SV)
The total PV booked to your account in one calendar month, whether you bought it or one of your customers did.
Commissionable Volume (CV)
80 percent of your SV, rounded to two decimals, and the only number any commission percentage is ever applied to.
Team Volume (TV)
The SV of everybody strictly below you, added up across your whole downline at every depth; it never includes your own SV.
Qualified month
A month in which your own SV reached 100.00 or more.
Active leg
A leg whose frontline Conductor is qualified this month; volume deeper in the leg never rescues it.
Rank
The title you hold for one month, recomputed from scratch each month, which sets how deep you are paid.
Paid depth
How many levels of your downline you are paid on: Member 1, Builder 2, Leader 3, Director 4, Executive 5.
Breakage
Commission that the percentages describe but nobody receives, because the person who would have received it failed a gate.
Commission run
One numbered, versioned batch calculation for one month, which produces every statement and is locked once it is final.

Team Volume excludes you. If your downline produced 2,500.00 of Sales Volume and you personally produced 200.00, your Team Volume is 2,500.00, not 2,700.00. Your own volume qualifies you; it never counts toward your own team requirement.

A leg is judged by its top person only. The plan asks whether your frontline Conductor is qualified, not whether the branch below them produced volume. That is deliberate: a rank is built by helping the people you personally sponsored stay active, not by sitting above one lucky branch.

Volume, and the eighty percent rule

The catalog is where volume comes from, so the catalog is where the plan starts. There are two tiers of agent, and volume equals dollars, one to one, always.

A subscription bills and grants its volume every month it stays active. Cancel it and the volume stops from the cancellation month forward; months already billed keep their volume.

The three volume numbers

  1. Personal Volume (PV) is printed on the product. The Payment Agent is 100 PV because it costs $100.00.
  2. Sales Volume (SV) is your month. Add the PV of everything booked to your account in one calendar month, your own subscriptions and your customers' subscriptions together.
  3. Commissionable Volume (CV) is 80 percent of your SV. It is the base for every percentage in this plan, without exception.

Read this twice, because it is the number one misreading of any plan of this kind.

Every percentage in this document is a percentage of Commissionable Volume, and Commissionable Volume is 80 percent of the money. So a 10 percent level 1 is not 10 percent of what the person spent. It is 10 percent of 80 percent, which is 8 percent of what they spent.

Worked in dollars: somebody you sponsored spends $150.00 this month. Their CV is 0.80 x 150.00 = 120.00. Your level 1 line is 0.10 x 120.00 = 12.00. As a fraction of their $150.00, that is 12.00 / 150.00 = 8 percent. The plan says this out loud here rather than letting anybody discover it on a statement later.

Why 80 and not 100

The 20 cents on every dollar that Commissionable Volume leaves behind never enters the commission calculation at all. It is the company's margin, decided before any percentage is applied. That is also why the plan can say truthfully that the ceiling on an order in the monthly commission run is 20 percent of the price: the five level percentages add to 25 percent of Commissionable Volume, and 25 percent of 80 percent is 20 percent.

That ceiling describes the monthly run, which is where every mechanism in sections seven, eight and nine is settled. The proposed Instant Payout in section ten would sit on top of it, on a first order only. See that section.

Section five

The qualified month: the single gate

Before any money is paid to anybody, one test runs. It is the only qualification test in the plan, and the same number does two separate jobs.

Qualified means your own Sales Volume reached 100.00 in that calendar month. That is the whole test. Exactly 100.00 qualifies; every threshold in this plan is "or more", never "more than".

Job one: it decides whether you can be paid

Commissions for a month are paid only to Conductors who were qualified in that month. An unqualified month means no commission lines are created for you at all. Not reduced, not deferred, and never made up later.

Job two: it decides whether your leg counts

Rank requirements count active legs, and your leg is active for your sponsor only when you are qualified. Your sponsor's rank therefore depends on you staying active, which is the whole reason sponsors help.

Ways to reach 100

  • One domain agent at 100 PV.
  • Two support agents at 50 PV each.
  • Any mix of agents that reaches 100.
  • Customer subscriptions booked to your account, which count exactly the same as your own.
  • Any combination of the above.

Qualification can rest on what you sell, not only on what you buy. A Conductor with no subscriptions of their own and two customers each holding one support agent has 100 SV and is fully qualified. This matters beyond the arithmetic: plans where the only route to qualification is buying your own product are the ones regulators call pay to play. This is the structural answer to that, and it is the reason the customer role exists at all.

If you do not qualify in a month

  1. You are paid nothing that month.
  2. Your leg does not count as active for your sponsor's rank, however much volume sits below you.
  3. You cannot hold any rank above Member, even if your Team Volume and leg counts would otherwise reach Leader or Director.
  4. Your own volume still pays your upline in full. Losing qualification costs you, not the people above you.

Qualification resets every month. There are no grace months in this version; a qualified March does nothing for April.

Section six

One organisation, every example

Every worked example from here to the end of the brochure comes out of the same ten person organisation, so you never have to learn a new cast. It is drawn once, here. The labels M1 to M10 are the same labels the plan specification uses, so anything here can be checked against it line by line.

Earning one

Your frontline, at ten percent

This is where most of the money in the plan is made, because level 1 pays 10 percent, twice the rate of any other level.

The rule. You are paid 10 percent of the Commissionable Volume of every Conductor you personally sponsored, for as long as you are qualified that month. Their own qualification does not matter. If they bought, you are paid on it.

Worked, every step

Take M2 and M6 out of the organisation. M2 sponsored M6. This month M6 subscribed to the Marketing Agent and the Customer Care agent.

  1. Marketing Agent, $100.00, 100 PV. Customer Care agent, $50.00, 50 PV.
  2. M6's Sales Volume for the month is 100 + 50 = 150.00.
  3. M6's Commissionable Volume is 0.80 x 150.00 = 120.00.
  4. M2 is qualified this month, and level 1 sits inside every rank's paid depth, so both gates pass.
  5. M2's commission line is 0.10 x 120.00 = 12.00.

That is $12.00 on $150.00 of buying, which is 8 percent of what M6 actually spent. Not 10 percent, because the 10 percent applies to Commissionable Volume.

The same rule when the person below is not qualified

M8 sponsored M10. M10 subscribed to the Secretary agent only.

  1. M10's Sales Volume is 50.00, so M10's Commissionable Volume is 0.80 x 50.00 = 40.00.
  2. M10 is not qualified, because 50.00 is below 100.00. That does not matter here.
  3. M8 is qualified, so M8's level 1 line is 0.10 x 40.00 = 4.00.

M10 earns nothing that month and M10's leg does not count as active for M8's rank, but M10's volume still pays M8 in full. That asymmetry is deliberate and it is written into the plan: all Commissionable Volume pays upline, whoever generated it.

Earning two

Depth, which rank unlocks

Levels 2 through 5 pay 5, 5, 3 and 2 percent of that person's Commissionable Volume, and you only reach them if your rank says you do. Paid depth is a hard cut: at depth 2 you are paid on levels 1 and 2 and you see nothing from level 3, however much volume is sitting there.

Worked, every step

M3 is a Builder, so M3's paid depth is 2. Underneath M3 sit M7 and M8 on the frontline, and M10 one level further down beneath M8.

  1. M7 subscribed to twelve domain agents and six support agents. Sales Volume = (12 x 100) + (6 x 50) = 1,500.00, so Commissionable Volume = 0.80 x 1,500.00 = 1,200.00.
  2. M8 subscribed to two support agents. Sales Volume = 100.00, Commissionable Volume = 80.00.
  3. M10 subscribed to one support agent. Sales Volume = 50.00, Commissionable Volume = 40.00.
  4. M7 is level 1 for M3: 0.10 x 1,200.00 = 120.00.
  5. M8 is level 1 for M3: 0.10 x 80.00 = 8.00.
  6. M10 is level 2 for M3: 0.05 x 40.00 = 2.00.
  7. M3's total is 120.00 + 8.00 + 2.00 = 130.00.

Now change one thing and nothing else. Suppose M3 held the rank of Member instead of Builder, so paid depth 1. M10 is out of reach. M3 earns 120.00 + 8.00 = 128.00, and the 2.00 is breakage. It is not pushed to the person above and it is not held for later. Nobody receives it.

The teaching point: rank buys reach, not rate. Going from Member to Builder does not increase what you earn on your frontline by a single cent. It opens a new row of people underneath them.

Earning three

Customer sales rolling up

Anybody can subscribe to Orvanna agents as a customer of a referring Conductor, without joining the tree. That order books onto the Conductor's account, tagged as a customer purchase with the customer's own identity kept on it so the receipt stays theirs.

The rule. The full Personal Volume of a customer purchase counts in the referring Conductor's Sales Volume. The customer never qualifies, never holds a rank, and never earns. In this version the referring Conductor does not receive a separate retail commission on the sale; the benefit is that the volume counts toward their own qualification, feeds their Commissionable Volume, and feeds their upline's Team Volume.

Worked, every step

M4 has no subscriptions at all this month. Two of M4's customers each subscribe to one support agent: one takes the Quality Assurance (QA) agent, the other takes the Secretary agent.

  1. QA agent $50.00, 50 PV. Secretary agent $50.00, 50 PV.
  2. Both orders book to M4's account, so M4's Sales Volume is 50 + 50 = 100.00.
  3. 100.00 reaches the gate, so M4 is qualified this month without having bought anything personally.
  4. M4's Commissionable Volume is 0.80 x 100.00 = 80.00.
  5. M4 earns nothing directly on these two sales. M4's sponsor, M1, earns 0.10 x 80.00 = 8.00 at level 1.
  6. Because M4 is now qualified, M4's leg counts as active for M1's rank, and M4 is eligible to be paid on anybody in M4's own downline.

Earning four

Instant Payout proposed terms

PROPOSED TERMS AWAITING APPROVAL. NOT YET BUILT.

Instant Payout has been adopted as the fourth way a Conductor earns, and the terms set out on this page are the recommended package. They have not been approved, and no software computes them yet. Nothing in this section is available today. Nobody should describe Instant Payout to a prospective Conductor as though it already pays, and no figure below should be quoted as settled policy.

Every other payout in this plan resolves once, at the end of a calendar month. Instant Payout is the exception by design: it pays on the day, because the gap between doing the work and seeing the reward is what stops most new Conductors before they start.

Read this sentence carefully, because Instant Payout breaks the rule the rest of this brochure taught you. Everywhere else, a percentage sits on Commissionable Volume, which is 80 percent of the money. Instant Payout is the one exception: its 20 percent sits on the order price itself. On a $150.00 first order that is 0.20 x 150.00 = $30.00, not 0.20 x 120.00 = $24.00. A reader who assumes the usual basis will underestimate this mechanism by a quarter.

Worked, every step

You sponsor somebody. Nineteen days after they enrol, they place their first order, a $150.00 subscription. You have bought agents of your own before, and this is your first Instant Payout this month.

  1. The order price is $150.00, which is below the $250.00 basis cap, so the whole price counts.
  2. Instant Payout = 0.20 x 150.00 = 30.00, paid to you the day the payment succeeds.
  3. At month end the ordinary plan still runs. Their Commissionable Volume is 0.80 x 150.00 = 120.00.
  4. Your level 1 line is 0.10 x 120.00 = 12.00, exactly as it would have been without Instant Payout.
  5. Total to you on that first order: 30.00 + 12.00 = 42.00.

How the cap behaves

Suppose the first order had been $400.00 instead. The basis is capped at $250.00, so the Instant Payout is 0.20 x 250.00 = 50.00, which is the most any single Instant Payout can ever pay. The level 1 commission at month end is unaffected by the cap and is still calculated on the full order.

One consequence to be honest about. Section four of this brochure says the most a single order can pay through the monthly run is 20 percent of its price. That remains true of the monthly run. If Instant Payout is approved, a Conductor's first order would additionally carry up to 20 percent of its price paid on the day, so a first order could pay more than 20 percent in total. Every other order in the plan is unaffected.

Section eleven

The rank ladder

Five rungs. Each one adds a level of reach, and nothing else. The percentages never change.

RankEverything below must hold in the same monthReach
Memberenrolled1 level
Builderqualified, and 2 or more active legs2 levels
Leaderqualified, Team Volume 2,500.00 or more, and 3 or more active legs3 levels
Directorqualified, Team Volume 10,000.00 or more, and 2 or more legs each containing a Builder or higher4 levels
Executivequalified, Team Volume 40,000.00 or more, and 2 or more legs each containing a Leader or higher5 levels

"A leg containing a Builder" means anybody inside that leg, at any depth, held Builder or higher this month.

The rules that govern the ladder

  • Every requirement on a rung must hold in the same month. There is no mixing of a good January with a good March.
  • You hold the highest rung you meet. Nobody is stuck on a lower rung because of the order things are evaluated in.
  • Ranks recompute from scratch every month. There is no retention, no highest ever achieved title, and no grace period. You are what you did this month.
  • Every threshold is inclusive. Exactly 100.00 qualifies. Exactly 2,500.00 of Team Volume makes Leader.
  • Every rank above Member requires your own qualified month. A large team alone never carries a title.

The top two rungs are counted differently, on purpose. For Leader you count active legs, which asks whether your frontline is qualified. For Director you count legs that contain a Builder or higher anywhere inside, and for Executive, legs that contain a Leader or higher. That is what makes the top of this ladder about developing other leaders rather than about accumulating volume.

Where the rest of the money went

The structural ceiling is 25 percent of Commissionable Volume across five levels. On 2,160.00 that would describe 540.00. Only 264.00 was paid, and every dollar of the difference has a reason.

Section thirteen

The calendar, rounding, and finality

How a month closes

  1. Every order is stamped with the month it counts toward at the moment it is created, in Coordinated Universal Time (UTC). Subscription orders always stamp the first day of the month. The stamp never moves afterward, so a late edit can never shift volume between months.
  2. Cutoff is the end of the calendar month. Nothing enters a closed month.
  3. The run computes volumes, qualification, ranks, and every commission line for that month.
  4. Finalisation locks the run. A final run's statements cannot be edited, deleted, or quietly added to.
  5. A recomputation never edits history. It creates a new numbered run, and the old one stays on record marked superseded. Every statement traces to the exact run that produced it.

How the arithmetic is rounded

Rounding happens in exactly two places and nowhere else:

  1. Once per Conductor per month, when Commissionable Volume is computed as 0.80 x Sales Volume.
  2. Once per commission line, when the rate is applied to the source Commissionable Volume.

Rounding is half up, so at exactly half a cent the Conductor gets the extra cent. Totals are never rounded twice. Your statement total is the sum of your already rounded lines, and the company total is the sum of the already rounded statements. That is what stops a statement from failing to add up.

With the current catalog, rounding never actually fires: every price is a multiple of $50.00, so every Commissionable Volume is a multiple of 40, and every rate lands exactly on a cent.

Same data, same plan version, same answer. The organisation is photographed once at the start of a run and every later step reads that photograph, so editing the tree while a run is in flight cannot change that run's output. Every figure is fixed point decimal, never floating point. Run the same month twice and it agrees to the cent.

How many levels of your downline you are paid on, set by your rank: Member 1, Builder 2, Leader 3, Director 4, Executive 5.
Personal Volume (PV)
The volume figure carried by a product, which at Orvanna always equals its price in dollars.
Qualified month
A month in which your own Sales Volume reached 100.00 or more. The single gate for being paid and for your leg counting as active.
Rank
The title you hold for one month, recomputed from scratch each month, which sets your paid depth. The five ranks are Member, Builder, Leader, Director and Executive.
Sales Volume (SV)
The total Personal Volume booked to your account in one calendar month, whether you bought it or one of your customers did.
Sponsor
The one Conductor directly above you, the person who enrolled you.
Team
The Conductors you sponsored and everybody below them. The team drives Team Volume and rank. It is never the word for your agents.
Team Volume (TV)
The Sales Volume of everybody strictly below you, added across your whole downline at every depth. It never includes your own Sales Volume.
Upline
Everybody above you: your sponsor, their sponsor, and so on to the top of the tree.
Volume month
The calendar month an order counts toward, stamped when the order is created and never moved afterward.
